## Description to copy

This methods report brings together finite-proper-reply-delay and unequal-speed kinematic exercises with an explicitly assumed fixed-preferred-time free scalar action. The action changes the reciprocal sender-frame propagation rule. Its source-free preferred Hamiltonian is nonnegative and its free frequencies are real under the stated signs and mass assumptions. Retarded propagation respects one common global time, so the positive loop-severity diagnostic vanishes for populations supported on this particular prescription. This is a limitation of the diagnostic's assumptions, not physical evidence for FTL or a universal speed conclusion.

The report distinguishes preferred-slice energy and transformed physical modes from formal canonical flux and arbitrary data on a non-Cauchy boosted surface. It derives a complete-continuum retarded front and conditional laboratory roundtrip anisotropy. The front conclusion depends on validity of the free operator to arbitrarily high frequency; an unspecified effective-theory ultraviolet completion does not settle a physical front. Source/detector interactions, quantum/gravitational completion, ordinary matter coupling, signal-production energy and an empirical PTA/ringdown bridge remain unsupplied. Laboratory formulas assume controllable pulses and calibrated apparatus; no observation or experiment is reported.

Original standard-library scripts, disclosed registration/amendments, hash-bound results, exact rational checks, independent geometric routes and four rejected in-memory defects accompany the free-model derivations. These are internal conditional mathematical and synthetic checks. They do not constitute blind preregistration, external peer review, novelty or a physically realized hidden-sector theory. Three exact-version primary sources are compared with explicit limits; citation metadata and original summaries are included, while downloaded article contents are excluded.

The preceding finite-delay and unequal-speed threshold/cap mathematics, exact direction-swap examples, historical documentation, dated public Zenodo-family verification and curated synthetic public-toolkit validation remain included.
